Test Whether Your Customer's Domains Can Actually Be Spoofed

Most phishing tools refuse to model the attack that drives the most damaging social-engineering incidents: spoofing the customer's own domain. The PhishU Framework auto-scores every target domain for SPF, DMARC, DKIM, and the receiver-side mail gateway, then offers viable targets directly in the sender dropdown with no SMTP, DNS, or relay configuration on the operator's side.

Automatic posture scoring

Recon grades every target domain for SPF, DMARC (policy plus pct), DKIM presence and key size, and the receiver-side mail gateway (Proofpoint, Mimecast, Barracuda, Cisco, Trend Micro, Sophos). Refreshed on every recon pass with no operator action.

Spoofable targets surface in the dropdown

When a domain's published authentication is weak enough that a realistic attacker would land mail, the domain appears as a sender option in the existing Email Settings dropdown. Pick it from a menu. The Framework auto-routes that campaign through a dedicated outbound relay.

Customer remediation in every report

Every customer report includes a graphical Email Security Posture Report Card grading each target domain with the exact DNS records to publish, ready to paste into a zone file. The lowest-graded domain is called out as the priority remediation target.

No SMTP config

No relay provisioning, no DKIM key generation, no relayhost setup. The dedicated outbound infrastructure is maintained centrally.

No DNS work

Operators do not own the spoofed domain. There is nothing to publish on the operator side. Authorization rides on the existing Campaign Targets domain-validation flow.

Header-inspection training

When the technique is used in a campaign, the recipient training automatically includes a slide on inbound header inspection: reading Authentication-Results, finding the real sending IP, and what DNS the org should publish.

The realism gap every other platform leaves open

Every commercial phishing simulation tool restricts senders to a verified pool. Operator-owned via a DNS challenge, or platform-owned via an in-house domain inventory. Either way, the platform itself attests to the sender domain. That structural anti-abuse property keeps the platform safe to operate against any target. It also keeps it blind to supplier-impersonation, the single most common social-engineering pattern of the last decade.

The PhishU Framework now closes that gap inside the same product used for landing pages, AiTM, reporting, and training. One platform, one report, one training story.

What makes this different

Operators do not stitch together a sender domain, an outbound relay, a DNS posture audit, a remediation checklist, and a header-inspection training slide as separate pieces of work. PhishU produces all of it from one campaign run.

  • Recon posture scoring is automatic and refreshed by the platform
  • The sender dropdown is the only operator UI; eligible domains appear there
  • The outbound relay infrastructure and IP reputation are managed centrally
  • The customer report contains exact DNS records to publish, not generic advice
  • Training is included and conditionally generated when the technique was used

Honest about where it works

A domain only qualifies as spoofable when the published authentication is actually weak. Domains with SPF -all, DMARC p=reject, and an enforcing receiver-side gateway are correctly scored low and never appear as a target. This is the design: PhishU tests domains that an attacker could already spoof today, and the same scoring engine produces the customer's posture report so they know which of their domains an attacker could not.

Customers find out which of their domains are protected and which are not, both with the same engagement.
